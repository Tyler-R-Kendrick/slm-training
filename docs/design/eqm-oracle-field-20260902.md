## What is computed

Equilibrium Matching (arXiv:2510.02300) trains `f(x_γ) ≈ (ε − x)·c(γ)` with
`x_γ = γx + (1−γ)ε`, `γ ~ U(0,1)`, `ε ~ N(0, I_d)`. Because
`ε − x = (x_γ − x)/(1−γ)` exactly, the perfect-training field is the conditional
expectation `f*(x̂) = λ·E[c(γ)/(1−γ)·(x̂ − x) | x_γ = x̂]`. For a finite dataset
`{x_i}` this is closed-form: the posterior over `(i, γ)` is
`∝ exp(−‖x̂ − γx_i‖²/(2(1−γ)²) − d·log(1−γ))`, evaluated as a softmax over `i` and
a trapezoid quadrature over a composite `u = 1−γ` grid (uniform in γ on
`[0, 0.95]` plus log-spaced `u` down to `u_min`). Paper defaults are used
throughout: `c_trunc(a=0.8)`, `λ = 4`, GD `x ← x − ηf(x)` with `η = 0.0017`
for 250 steps, adaptive stop `‖f‖ < g_min`. All datasets use unit-RMS
coordinates (`‖x_i‖ ≈ √d`), so the antipodal modes are well separated at every `d`.

Budget reductions to fit the 60 s cap (stated per item): Monte-Carlo uses 1024
trajectories at `d ∈ {2, 64}` and 256 at `d = 4096`; the GD sweeps and the
equilateral scan use a 1024+128-node γ grid with `u_min = 1e-3` (the quadrature
self-check at the end bounds the resulting field error at ≤0.6 %), while the
midpoint items use 16384+2048 nodes with `u_min = 1e-6`; the equilateral scan is a
121×121 in-plane grid with Newton refinement of every grid-local minimum of `‖f‖`;
orthogonal second derivatives use three random directions. No plots (matplotlib is
not a dependency); tables only.

## Reading

- The external report's midpoint zero is real but is a **saddle**, not a local
  minimum, at every dimension tested; Statement 2 is wrong only in its sloppy
  definition ("local minimum … `f(x̂) = 0`"), not in the property it is about.
- "Extended near-zero-field regions where GD stalls" is backwards in high `d`:
  along the axis the window is `~1/d` wide and orthogonally the field is a
  restoring spring (`∂²E ≈ E[g∣x̂] > 6`), so a Gaussian init has no measurable
  probability of stopping there once `d ≥ 64`.
- The oracle field near data is dimension-independent (exact zero at `x_i`,
  stiffness `λ/(1−a)` beside it), so N7's `d^(−1/2)` story for two-moons is closed:
  a low-`d` failure has to be blamed on the trained network or on the sampler's
  loose stop, both of which this exact-field study cannot see.
- Closed-form oracle fields make every EqM theory claim testable without
  training; this script is the reusable instrument for that.
